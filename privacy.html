1. Who is responsible

For this demonstration, the person presenting the site is Aleksandar Kehayov, trading as UX UI MATE (“we”, “us”). Contact: uxuimate.com.

The mailbox shown on the marketing pages is hello@halden.com. That address is part of the example. A live operator would name a legal entity, a postal address, and, where required, a data protection officer.

If Halden is later sold as a real product to clinics, the clinic is typically the controller of patient call data. The operator of Halden would act as a processor under Article 28 GDPR, on the clinic’s instructions, under a written data-processing agreement.

2. What this demonstration actually does

This static website does not operate a live telephone agent. In the form you see today:

  • the start form and the “Get Halden” modal validate fields in your browser and then show a thank-you state;
  • the contents of the form are not sent to a server from this demonstration;
  • your theme choice (light or dark) is stored in your browser under the key halden-theme;
  • we do not set advertising cookies and we do not run a third-party analytics tag in this example.

The rest of this policy describes the standard position that would apply if the same product were operated for real in 2026. Read it as the intended live standard, not as a claim that those processes are running now.

3. Data we would collect in a live service

Website and enquiries

  • name, practice or business name, work email, telephone number;
  • the message you type (for example, the call you do not want guessed);
  • technical logs that a host may create (IP address, browser type, date and time) if a live host is used.

Subscription and billing

  • account and billing contacts, location count, payment references;
  • invoices and tax data required by law.

The telephone service

  • caller numbers, called numbers, time and duration;
  • audio, live transcript, summary, and notes passed to a human who joins;
  • booking data written into the calendar you connect;
  • language detected or selected during the call.

Call audio about a patient’s health is special-category data under Article 9 GDPR. A live operator would process it only with a valid condition (usually the clinic’s healthcare or explicit-consent basis) and appropriate safeguards.

4. Legal bases (GDPR, 2026)

Depending on the activity, a live operator would rely on:

  • Contract (Article 6(1)(b)) — to set up and run the service you buy;
  • Legitimate interests (Article 6(1)(f)) — to keep the site secure, prevent abuse, and improve reliability, balanced against your rights;
  • Legal obligation (Article 6(1)(c)) — tax, accounting, and lawful requests;
  • Consent (Article 6(1)(a) and, where needed, Article 9(2)(a)) — optional marketing, and any cookie that is not strictly necessary;
  • Public interest / healthcare conditions — only where the clinic, as controller, has a lawful basis to handle health data and instructs us as processor.

You may withdraw consent at any time. Withdrawal does not affect processing that already happened.

5. How data is used

  • to reply to an enquiry or start a subscription;
  • to answer and route calls, book appointments, and hand a call to a human with context;
  • to bill, account, and prevent fraud;
  • to meet EU transparency rules for AI systems, including telling a caller they are speaking to an automated system where the Artificial Intelligence Act or other 2026 law requires it;
  • to improve quality, using aggregated or otherwise safeguarded data where the law allows;
  • to defend legal claims.

We would not sell personal data. We would not use enquiry or call content for unrelated advertising.

6. Calls, transcripts and AI

A live Halden service would use automated speech recognition and language models to understand a call and to draft a summary. In 2026 that processing must stay inside the purpose you agreed and the clinic’s instructions.

Models would not be trained on your identifiable call content unless a live contract said so in plain language and a lawful basis existed. Human operators who join a call would see the transcript and a short summary so the caller does not start again.

You (the practice) must tell callers what your profession and local law require — for example that calls may be recorded or that an automated system may answer.

7. Who data is shared with

Only where needed:

  • hosting, telephony and calendar providers acting as processors under contract;
  • payment providers for paid plans;
  • human operators who join a call;
  • professional advisers under confidentiality;
  • authorities when the law requires it.

Each processor would be bound to use the data only on documented instructions, to keep it secure, and not to keep it longer than needed.

8. How long data is kept

  • theme preference — until you clear site data;
  • enquiry messages in a live inbox — typically up to 24 months after the last contact, unless a contract or claim needs them longer;
  • billing records — the period required by tax law (often 7 to 10 years in EU Member States);
  • call recordings and transcripts — the period set with the clinic (a live default would be stated in the order; many practices choose 30 to 90 days unless a complaint or legal hold applies).

When the period ends, data is deleted or irreversibly anonymised.

9. International transfers

If a live service stored or accessed data outside the European Economic Area, transfers would use a 2026-valid tool: an adequacy decision, the European Commission’s standard contractual clauses then in force, or another Article 46 GDPR mechanism, plus any transfer assessment the law still requires.

This demonstration is a static site. Viewing it in your browser does not, by itself, send form contents abroad.

10. Your rights

If GDPR applies to you, you may ask to:

  • access your data and receive a copy;
  • correct inaccurate data;
  • erase data, where the conditions in Article 17 are met;
  • restrict or object to certain processing;
  • receive data you provided in a portable format (Article 20);
  • withdraw consent;
  • not be subject to a solely automated decision with legal or similarly significant effects, unless Article 22 allows it — call routing that a human can take over is designed to avoid that outcome.

Use the contact details below. We would answer within one month, or explain a lawful extension. You may also complain to your local supervisory authority. In the EU you can find the authority via the European Data Protection Board.

If the clinic is the controller of your call, send the request to the clinic as well. We would help them as processor.

11. Cookies and local storage

This demonstration uses local storage for one strictly necessary preference: light or dark theme. That is not a tracking cookie. No consent banner is shown because no non-essential cookie is set.

A live marketing site that later adds analytics or advertising cookies would show a consent choice that meets the ePrivacy rules as they apply in 2026, and would record that choice.

12. Children

This website is aimed at adult practice owners and staff. We do not knowingly collect data from children through this demonstration. A live phone agent may speak to a parent or guardian about a child; that data would be handled under the clinic’s instructions and healthcare rules, not as a service offered to the child.

13. Security

A live operator would use appropriate technical and organisational measures: access control, encryption in transit, least-privilege staff access to transcripts, logging, and a process for personal-data breaches (including notification under Articles 33 and 34 GDPR where required).

No method is perfect. You should not send passwords or payment-card numbers through the message box on this demonstration.

14. Changes

We may update this policy by publishing a new version on this page and changing the date at the top. Material changes to a live service would be notified to account holders by email where we have an address.
